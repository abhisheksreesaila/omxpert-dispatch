Can a 125B MoE run well on a desktop?

Can we optimise Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B mixture of experts, the Qwen 4 preview architecture) for local AI? Today that means 2× RTX 3090 (48 GB of GPU memory) + 64 GB RAM. Could smaller setups work too?

The idea. Mixture of experts is the baseline now: nearly every new model is one, and each token wakes only a few of its thousands of experts.

So why carry every generic expert at full cost? Choose our own experts: keep the ones my work actually uses, specialised and personal, close to the GPU, and make the rest cheap.

Standing on the shoulders of giants

What we borrowed, and from whom.

  • 0xSero · glm-flash-liteThe systems tricks: parallel read pool, layer-ahead prefetch, exclusive VRAM/RAM tiers, staged prefill, CPU/GPU overlap, no per-layer host sync (Days 4–6).
  • FreeToken (FlashML) + FreeToken-KaiThe hybrid idea: the CPU computes cold experts while the GPU computes hot ones (Day 6, and the core of the Mojo layer). Fork: yuuki-net/FreeToken-Kai.
  • llama.cpp / ggmlThe engine every run used, and the custom-op hook the Mojo expert layer plugs into.
  • The Qwen teamThe model, its MTP draft head and the n-gram lookup table.
  • unslothThe GGUF quants (UD-Q4_K_XL) that fit 125B on this desktop.
  • Modular · Mojo and MAXThe language and compiler of the Day 7 expert layer (GPU and CPU from one source).
  • ReMoE · CAR-MoE · melinoeRouter-locality training: the plan for the reward-trained router. Also CAR-MoE, melinoe, and the honest negative result on locality vs quality.
  • REAP (Cerebras)Router-weighted expert pruning from calibration data: the plan for deleting experts my work doesn't use.
  • MoLE · Mixture of Lookup ExpertsExperts as lookup tables that live off the GPU: the plan for CPU lookups.
  • Hugging Face · harness adaptersThe recipe for reward training: GRPO + rank-32 LoRA + an efficiency bonus in the reward.
  • The prior-art listExpert offloading and placement before us: ProMoE, MoE-Infinity, PowerInfer, KTransformers, Cache-Prior, DoMoE and more.
