The vision

Local AI shouldn't need a data centre.

The smartest open models are mixtures of experts, and they're huge. Today you either rent them, or you shrink them until they forget things. We want the third option: run the real model on the machine you already own.

Yours

Your data stays home

Code, notes and chats never leave the laptop. No account, no rate limit, no one else's terms.

Whole

The full intelligence

The model as released, not a distilled cousin. When a hard question needs every expert, they're on the SSD.

Personal

Shaped by your work

Most of what one person does is four or five kinds of task. The machine learns which experts those use.

The idea

Every token uses 4 of 128 experts. Yours use the same few.

Below is one layer of the model: 128 experts. Your profile's experts sit in fast memory (teal). When the router wants one that isn't loaded, it waits on the SSD (orange). Turn on steering and the router takes a loaded neighbour instead.

What did the work

Pin. Steer. Batch.

We tried more than forty ideas. Three levers did almost all of the work, and they stack.

Pin

32%
faster · exact output · 2×RTX 3090

Keep the session profile's experts in the fastest memory, instead of llama.cpp's default split by layer.

Steer

2.85×
gpt-oss-120b · +8.3% perplexity

Nudge the router toward experts already loaded. One dial, λ. At λ=0.5 it's 1.46× for +2.0%.

Batch

7.0
tok/s total · 8 jobs · steered

Independent jobs share each layer's expert reads. Steering herds them onto the same experts, so batching pays.

Personal profiles

+11 points

On a 256-expert model, a profile from your own coding beats one from your other work at a 25% cache, and nearly doubles it at 6%.

Right profile

+24%

Loading the profile that matches the session pays. The wrong one gives nothing. A live swap takes 0.45 s.

Profiles age

25–28%

That much of the coding hot set changed within weeks. Refresh profiles from recent sessions.

What failed

The ideas that didn't survive measurement.

0.27–0.75×

Speculative decoding

Even at 69% acceptance. Checking k tokens costs about k tokens, because consecutive tokens pick different experts.

Out of memory

Two models at once

A small chat model beside the 120B crashed the GPU. In 16 GB, the two lanes take turns.

0 gain

Skip the missing expert

Dropping it cost as much quality as steering and gave no speed. A nearby expert beats no expert.

= one dial

A learned router bias

A trained bias per expert landed on the same trade-off curve as a single hand-set λ.

Lost

Plain pinning on a Mac

macOS's page cache already keeps hot pages in memory. Steering is the lever on unified memory.

1.44×

Batching without steering

With 4 of 128 experts per token, separate jobs rarely share experts until steering pulls them together.

Where it points

A fast lane to talk. A deep lane to work.

Understands and plans

  • Names the task: coding, search, tools, docs
  • Writes the work as a batch of independent jobs
  • Steps aside: 16 GB can't hold both

Work lane · gpt-oss-120b

Full intelligence, batched

  • Loads that task's experts (pin)
  • Steers toward them, λ set per model
  • Runs the batch together (batch)
  • ~7 tok/s total while you're away

A steering-aware adapter
