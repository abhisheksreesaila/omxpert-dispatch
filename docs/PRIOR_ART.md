# Expert Dispatch: prior art (checked 2026-11-04)

Pieces under test: **(a)** a per-user offline routing profile that drives VRAM/RAM/SSD placement, **(b)** a learned predictor that prefetches upcoming experts, **(c)** overnight self-tuning: re-profile, re-place, bias or fine-tune the router toward resident experts.
Numbers are the authors' own claims. Rows marked * use figures from memory or secondary sources that I did not re-check.

## 2. Verdict per piece

- **(a) Per-user offline profile for placement: partially done.** The mechanics are already shipped: record activation stats on *your* workload, then pin hot experts (PowerInfer, the KTransformers `frequency` strategy, MoE-Infinity traces, DoMoE's historical tables, EASY-EP's domain demos, the neurall fork's persisted placement). Nobody frames it as "one person's corpus over weeks", and nobody measures whether one person's routing is skewed and stable enough to beat a plain online cache. That measurement is open. The bar to beat is now mainline llama.cpp's LRU cache, at 72-89% hits.
- **(b) Learned predictor for prefetch: already done, and has weak returns on this hardware.** At least eight papers do this (ProMoE, ExpertFlow, DoMoE, FlashMoE, fMoE, eMoE, Pre-gated, AdapMoE). HOBBIT argues that loading cost swamps prediction gains. The neurall fork's learned predictor gave **no gain on 2x RTX 3090**. Little is left here unless the predictor serves the SSD tier, where the lead time is larger.
- **(c) Overnight self-tuning: partially done.** Inference-time bias toward cached experts exists (Cache-Prior), and so do offline router fine-tunes for locality (ReMoE, CAR-MoE). Re-profiling and re-placing on a schedule is just engineering. **Not found anywhere:** a *per-user, nightly* router fine-tune or prune on the user's own logged data with a quality gate, or any use of idle time for this.
- **The combination: not found as one system.** The nearest are the neurall fork (persistence, cache and a learned predictor on the owner's exact GPU pair) and DoMoE (history-driven tables). As a product it is new. As research its novelty is thin unless (a) and (c) are measured on real personal workloads.

## 3. Three most promising open angles for this owner

1. **Prove or kill the premise cheaply: measure personal routing skew and drift.** Log routing for 1-2 weeks of real use (code, chat, docs). Measure how concentrated it is (what share of experts gets 90% of hits) and how stable it is day to day. Then benchmark on the 2x3090: a profile-pinned warm cache plus LRU against merged `--moe-cache-mib` LRU alone. A concrete upstream lever: **persist and warm-start the #29887 cache from a profile file**. #29887 is LRU, in-memory and single-device; #30112 covers multi-GPU. If the warm start does not beat LRU after the first minute, drop (a).
2. **A nightly, per-user router-only fine-tune (ReMoE-style) gated by quality.** ReMoE shows 1.77-1.99x decode from generic locality tuning. Nobody has tuned on *one user's* logged prompts with an anchor loss and an automatic check: perplexity and task evals on the user's held-out data, rolled back if worse. This fits the "while you sleep" story, is cheap (router weights only) and is reversible.
3. **Profile-driven three tiers that let a bigger model fit in 64GB RAM.** Use EASY-EP/REAP-style saliency on the user's corpus to send cold experts to NVMe instead of pruning them. A fallback path (cold expert loaded from SSD, or swapped for a resident expert as in BuddyMoE/Cache-Prior) keeps quality. The win is running a model larger than RAM (for example ~100-200GB quantized) at usable speed, which static `-ot` cannot do. The predictor from (b) pays off only here, because SSD lead times are long.
